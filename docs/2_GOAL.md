# The Goal

**Make an open, self-contained research-and-playground project about
occlusion-tolerant segmented displays: a layout-parametric engine that computes
exact capacities, maximal-set counts, construction rules and character-set
limits for 7-segment, larger segment spaces and pixel grids; a reproducible
math note; and an objective, calibratable model of what makes a glyph
recognisable, including its occluded appearance.**

It must be useful, novel, and fun.  Re-proving known Hamming-graph results is
not the point; the point is the exact enumeration, the construction grammar,
the capacity consequences, the recognisability model, and the interactive
explanation that comes out of them.

Source: **EXPLICIT** — the user’s briefs of 2026-10-28, read together:
“这个项目就是完全 open 的”, “先不要推进阶段二，先把阶段一做扎实”, “把格子的
拓展和这个字符集的拓展本身先做进阶段一”, “记得写文档”, and the request that
recognisability include the occluded appearance with a lower weight.

Mode: **open** (the Goal is a direction; each milestone is a release).

## Priorities

1. **Correct and reproducible.**  Every number is generated by a script; the
   docs point to the script.  A claim that cannot be reproduced is not shipped.
2. **General by construction.**  The 7-segment results are one instance of a
   layout/cover model.  A new grid or character set must be data, not a rewrite.
3. **Novel or useful.**  Do not re-derive known graph theory.  The exact counts,
   the 9/10 gap, the capacity corollary, the recognisability model and the
   playable explanation are the candidates for value.
4. **Honest about perception.**  “Looks like” is a model until it is calibrated
   against people; the docs say which is which.
5. **Fun.**  The interactive lab/game is not decoration: it is how a
   non-specialist sees the problem and how the recognition data can be collected.

## Non-goals

- A production traffic-light font or a hardware certification.
- A full perceptual study in M1.
- A general theorem for every graph product; the open questions are recorded.
